\begin{abstract}
The leading soft-photon Ward kernel for a massive charge at a known
speed is a convolution operator on the sphere. We calculate its exact
large-degree spectral asymptotic and prove a sharp deterministic
minimax recovery rate for a signed angular charge density in a
Sobolev ball. The rate is logarithmic in the inverse noise level. For
two possible speeds, unrestricted signed densities need not be
identifiable from angular data alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The leading soft-photon theorem in QED relates a measured soft
insertion to a signed sum over hard charged particles. For massive
particles that angular dependence is smooth rather than concentrated
at a point on the sphere. Campiglia and Laddha give the relevant
hyperboloid kernel in their formulation of the leading Ward identity
\cite{massive-qed}. This motivates an inverse question: what can a
noisy angular observation determine about the distribution of hard
charge at one fixed speed?

The theorem below identifies the smoothing strength using the actual
kernel and gives matching upper and lower bounds in the same
observation norm. The data class consists of signed angular densities
with bounded Sobolev norm. A fixed speed is part of the prior, and
the two-speed example specifies exactly how dropping this assumption
can change identifiability.
